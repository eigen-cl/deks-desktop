# Story and evidence

## Frame the assignment

Before mutating a deck, state:

- audience and current knowledge;
- decision or behavior the presentation should cause;
- delivery format and approximate duration;
- narrative spine in one sentence;
- evidence standard and source date.

Do not make this planning frame into a slide unless it belongs in the requested story.

## Structure the deck

For every new presentation or complete rewrite:

1. Start with a minimal cover containing the available title, as-of date, author or presenter, and logo or brand.
2. Begin the story on the next checkpoint by establishing context: the current situation, actor, observed behavior, evidence, or question the audience needs before evaluating a solution.
3. Introduce the proposal only after that context is legible. Product features, architecture, and demos are proposal evidence, not substitutes for context.

The cover does not count as context. If the author, date, or brand is unavailable, ask for it or mark it as pending; do not infer it.

## Choose a narrative form

Use literary and analytical genres as structures of reasoning, not decorative labels. Choose one dominant form and adapt it to the assignment instead of mixing every pattern:

- **Report** — situation, evidence, finding, options, proposal, decision.
- **Essay** — context, thesis, arguments, objection, synthesis, proposal.
- **Human story** — scenario, actor, goal, friction, consequence, revelation, proposal, resolution.
- **Investigation** — symptom, facts, hypothesis, cause, proposal.

A secondary form may provide a concrete thread inside the dominant one. For example, an executive report can follow one employee and one document without abandoning evidence, options, and a decision close.

## Build a checkpoint storyboard

First create a section map. For every section, record its narrative job, first and last checkpoint, persistent story identities, anchor geometry, local elements, and the explicit scene change that permits a re-anchor. A section is a coherent visual scene, not a new number on every slide.

For each checkpoint, record:

- the single focal idea;
- persistent elements and their changed state or geometry;
- entering and exiting elements;
- the one central motion on the edge into it;
- the spoken beat or audience question it answers;
- evidence required for its material claims.

After the required `cover -> context -> proposal` ordering is established, adapt tension, evidence, mechanism, alternatives, objections, and resolution to the selected narrative form. Preserve one visual world and add only what the audience needs at that moment.

Use section labels consistently: keep one section identity and copy through its checkpoints or show it only on the section opener. Do not imply that every checkpoint is a new section when several slides develop one continuous scene.

## Keep a claim ledger

Classify each material assertion as:

- **sourced fact** — supported by a current primary source;
- **inference** — reasoned from sources and labelled;
- **estimate** — method and uncertainty stated;
- **unresolved** — omit or visibly qualify before delivery.

Prefer official documentation, contractual terms, standards bodies, laws, and original research. Record retrieval dates for provider policies, product capabilities, laws, prices, and other changing claims.

Do not convert scoped statements into universal ones. Distinguish personal, business, enterprise, API, and workspace plans; training, processing, retention, human review, and audit; host hooks, MCP, plugins, proxies, DLP, CASB, and endpoint controls. Verify a claimed hook's host, event, timing, payload, scope, and blocking behavior.

## Cite visibly

DEKS has no native speaker-note or citation-metadata field. Put concise source notes on relevant checkpoints or use a visible sources checkpoint. Add a `link-button` only when a clickable HTTPS source benefits the audience.

## Close proposals with a decision

End with the specific approval requested and a bounded pilot that produces evidence for the next decision. Use only supplied or sourced values. Otherwise use qualitative criteria or mark a value unresolved; never manufacture specificity.
